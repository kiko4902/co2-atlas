          About this atlas

          The map shows fossil CO₂ emissions (coal, oil, gas, cement production and gas flaring) by country and year. They are territorial emissions: counted where they happen, not where the goods are consumed. Land-use change is not included.

          Reading the map

          • Fixed scale (default) uses the same six classes for every year, so a colour means the same amount in 1950 and in 2024. Use it to compare years.
          • Relative to year recalculates the classes for the selected year so that each holds about the same number of countries. Use it to compare countries within a year. The same colour then means different amounts in different years.
          • 0 Mt is a reported zero. No data means the dataset has no estimate for that year. No separate data (hatched) means the territory is not reported on its own.
          • Per capita shows tonnes of CO₂ per person. Values and populations come from the Global Carbon Project (UN population data). Very small populations with refineries or oil and gas production (for example Sint Maarten in the 1950s, Qatar or Curaçao) can reach tens or hundreds of tonnes; they fall into the open top class. The world average is the global total, including aviation and shipping, per person. Shares of the world use the global total, which includes international aviation and shipping. Those are not assigned to any country, so country shares add up to less than 100%.

          Territories

          The map shows what the Global Carbon Project reports separately. Kosovo, Greenland and the Caribbean Netherlands have their own series and their own colour. French overseas regions (French Guiana, Guadeloupe, Martinique, Réunion, Mayotte) and Monaco are included in France, Western Sahara in Morocco and the Vatican in Italy. These are drawn as “no separate data”. Somaliland and Northern Cyprus are shown as part of Somalia and Cyprus. Antarctica is not drawn but can be found through the search. Borders are drawn for data display and do not imply any position on disputed territories.
